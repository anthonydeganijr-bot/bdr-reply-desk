    The workflow, before and after

    Today: every reply is handled by hand. Open it, decide what it is, find or write the right template, fill it in, then open Salesforce to fix the contact and Gong to fix the flow. Every rep does it differently, and most keep a private spreadsheet to track it.

    With the desk: replies are handled in one daily batch.

    DeskSortClassifies each reply into one of 15 types with fixed rules, and highlights the sentence that triggered it.
    DeskAssemblePicks the template, pre-fills it, sets the flow action and lists the record cleanup.
    RepDecideReads, edits, can override the category, and approves. Nothing moves without this step.
    RepCallThe time saved goes to the phone, where district meetings get booked.

    One rule set, every vertical

    The workflow is the same whether the reply comes from a district office or a faculty member. The desk detects the vertical from the institution and adjusts the wording. The rules, guardrails and approval step don't change.

    • Higher ed: exam and desk copy requests, sabbaticals (treated as out of office, never do-not-contact), "I no longer teach this course" (the person stays active; only that course's outreach stops), adoption committees and course coordinators, and departments that switched platforms.
    • K-12: retirements with a named successor, "talk to curriculum," district pricing questions with student counts, and summer auto-replies.
    • Any vertical: bounces, opt-outs, out of office, interested, pricing, referrals and not interested work the same way. Adding a vertical means adding templates, not rebuilding anything.

    Hard lines built into the code

    • Nothing sends. The desk drafts; the rep sends.
    • No invented facts. A successor's name or email is used only if it appears word for word in the reply. If the reply says someone retired and names no one, no one is added. Tests check this.
    • Out of office is never do-not-contact. A leave of absence or sabbatical is treated as an auto-reply, even if it says "remove me." A faculty member who stops teaching a course stays an active contact.
    • Do-not-contact gets no reply, and it's removed from every flow, not just one.
    • Same input, same output. The rules are deterministic, so the desk can be audited and every decision can be explained.
    • No prospect data leaves the machine. The prototype runs in the browser on synthetic replies.

    Path to production

    1. Week 1-2, pilot as a checklist. The rep classifies first, then compares with the desk. Each disagreement either fixes a rule or captures knowledge nobody had written down.
    2. Week 3-4, inside the Pearson tenant. An Outlook rule feeds a folder. Power Automate runs the rules. A Teams approval card replaces this screen. Salesforce is the source of truth, because Gong can't write to the CRM.
    3. Then share it. The rules and templates are just a written standard, so every BDR and inside sales team in higher ed, K-12 or any other Pearson business gets the same triage, and a new hire gets it on day one.
